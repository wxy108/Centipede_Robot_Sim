# Diagnosing and Correcting Directional Displacement Bias in the Centipede Robot MuJoCo Model

**Technical causal-audit report**  
**Project:** Centipede Robot MuJoCo reliability and random-contact experiments  
**Scope:** Steps 20--26, consolidated on 27 August 2026

## Executive conclusion

The original observation was that the mean signed displacement under IID
random leg-contact commands was positive and increased with segment number.
The initial physical model showed a fitted slope of

\[
6.666\ \mathrm{mm/added\ segment}
\quad
\text{(95% CI: }[5.358,7.955]\text{ mm/segment)}.
\]

The audit establishes that this observation contained two distinct effects:

1. **A nonphysical numerical amplifier.** A one-ended floating root and the
   associated serial dynamics tree/contact-constraint ordering introduced a
   signed displacement bias whose amplitude increased with robot length.
   Re-rooting the same physical robot from the head and tail exposed this
   component directly. Its mean amplitude increased from 14.10 mm at \(N=4\)
   to 27.17 mm at \(N=10\), with a slope of
   \(2.277\,[2.135,2.418]\) mm per added segment.
2. **A residual physical-model directional component.** After center-rooting
   and endpoint-average debiasing, the real STL robot retained a positive
   displacement of approximately 22--37 mm, but no statistically resolved
   linear growth with \(N\). The center-root grand mean was
   \(32.75\,[27.76,37.76]\) mm, while its slope was
   \(0.607\,[-2.088,3.248]\) mm per added segment.

The properly constructed center-rooted, reflection-symmetric dynamic null
behaved differently: every per-\(N\) confidence interval contained zero, its
grand mean was \(1.54\,[-0.65,3.80]\) mm, and its slope was
\(0.044\,[-1.148,1.232]\) mm per added segment. The real center-rooted robot
exceeded that null by an across-\(N\) average of

\[
31.21\ \mathrm{mm}
\quad
\text{(95% bootstrap CI: }[25.73,36.70]\text{ mm)}.
\]

The original claim must therefore be revised as follows:

> The apparent monotonic displacement increase with segment number was
> primarily a dynamics-tree/contact-order artifact. Center-rooting removes
> that resolved trend. The real asymmetric robot nevertheless retains a
> nonzero directional displacement relative to the symmetric dynamic null,
> most plausibly because distributed fore-aft morphology and boundary
> asymmetry are rectified by tangential friction and intermittent contact.

## 1. Original problem and why it was surprising

The experiment retained a body-yaw wave and independently randomized each
physical left/right leg pitch state every half gait cycle:

\[
X_{i,s,k}\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(0.5),
\qquad
i\in\{1,\ldots,N\},\quad s\in\{L,R\}.
\]

Here \(X=1\) denotes the commanded pitch-down state and \(X=0\) the
pitch-up state. The commands are IID across segment, side, decision window,
and - in the corrected implementation - across segment count \(N\).

Because left and right were sampled symmetrically, the initial intuition was
that forward and backward impulses should cancel and the ensemble mean should
be zero. Instead, the original endpoint-rooted physical model produced a
positive mean that increased from approximately 51 mm at \(N=4\) to 92 mm at
\(N=10\).

That intuition contained an important category error. The contact state is an
availability gate, not a signed force:

\[
X\in\{0,1\},
\qquad
\mathbb E[X]=0.5,
\]

not a symmetric forward/backward variable in \(\{-1,+1\}\). Pitch-up removes
or weakens a contact opportunity; it does not automatically create the exact
negative of the pitch-down longitudinal impulse. A zero mean therefore
requires a stronger symmetry: there must exist a probability-preserving
transformation \(\mathcal S\) such that

\[
P(\mathcal S X)=P(X),
\qquad
D(\mathcal S X)=-D(X).
\]

For longitudinal displacement, the relevant transformation is a complete
head-tail mechanical reflection together with a dynamically equivalent
re-rooting. Left-right exchange alone reverses lateral motion, not
longitudinal motion. Bitwise up/down complementation is also not a dynamical
sign inversion because impacts, friction, and contact history are unilateral.

## 2. Causal decomposition used in the audit

The measured displacement was treated as a sum of conceptually distinct
terms:

\[
D_{\mathrm{obs}}(N)
=D_{\mathrm{morph}}(N)
+B_{\mathrm{root}}(N)
+B_{\mathrm{init}}
+B_{\mathrm{world}}
+B_{\mathrm{rng}}
+B_{\mathrm{solver,res}}
+\varepsilon.
\]

- \(D_{\mathrm{morph}}\): displacement generated by the retained physical
  geometry, inertial distribution, finite-chain boundaries, actuation, and
  frictional contact.
- \(B_{\mathrm{root}}\): first-order bias caused by choosing a privileged end
  of the articulated/contact tree.
- \(B_{\mathrm{init}}\): inherited momentum or phase bias introduced before
  the evaluation interval.
- \(B_{\mathrm{world}}\): a fixed preference for the world frame or floor.
- \(B_{\mathrm{rng}}\): reuse or nesting of random streams across \(N\).
- \(B_{\mathrm{solver,res}}\): residual dependence on the finite ordered
  nonlinear contact solve after the leading root bias is removed.
- \(\varepsilon\): finite-sample fluctuation.

The purpose of the investigation was not to force the real robot's
\(D_{\mathrm{obs}}\) to zero. It was to reject or quantify every
non-geometric term before interpreting the remaining displacement.

## 3. Investigation chronology

### 3.1 Contact loss under an all-down command

The first audit separated commanded pitch from measured contact. Even with
every leg commanded down, only about 77--80% of leg-time samples were in
contact. The non-contact clearance was generally about 0.01 mm and never above
0.2 mm. Thus the apparent contact loss was not a commanded swing: it was
near-threshold contact-set switching in a compliant, overconstrained
multi-contact system.

This finding matters because extension and contraction occur with different
active support sets. A reciprocal-looking body wave can therefore be
frictionally rectified into net translation.

### 3.2 World-frame preference was rejected

Rotating the whole model by \(180^\circ\) reversed world-frame displacement
but preserved robot-frame forward displacement. The selected direction was
attached to the robot, not to the checkerboard, gravity convention, or world
\(+y\) axis.

### 3.3 Initialization bias was rejected

Static controls and a neutral initialization protocol were tested. The robot
was settled in a straight, all-down, body-wave-off state, connected smoothly
to the experiment controller, and warmed up before measurement. In the final
physical-model audit, body-wave-off drift was at most

\[
2.21\times10^{-6}\ \mathrm m,
\]

which is negligible relative to the centimeter-scale IID results.

### 3.4 Chassis rubbing and foot-shape explanations were rejected

No non-foot ground contacts were observed in the causal audit. Disabling
non-leg collisions produced the same displacement. Replacing the STL foot by
a spherical point contact, moving that point to zero fore-aft offset, and
mirroring the offset all preserved the selected direction. Consequently,
chassis rubbing, foot-patch shape, and foot-tip offset are not the sign
selector.

### 3.5 Tangential friction was identified as necessary

Removing rolling/torsional friction or passive joint friction changed the
magnitude but not the sign. Removing tangential foot-ground friction reduced
the displacement to below one percent of its matched reference value. In the
primitive-null direction audit, frictionless ground removed more than 96% of
the nominal deterministic displacement.

The supported mechanism is therefore a frictional contact rectification, not
a ballistic or kinematic translation.

### 3.6 Chassis mass asymmetry was rejected as a sufficient cause

The mass first-moment audit showed that the chassis contribution was not the
dominant positive offset. At \(N=10\), shoulder and yaw-link placements
contributed approximately +6.56 mm and +6.01 mm to the COM offset,
respectively, whereas the chassis contribution was -0.41 mm. Centering only
the chassis barely changed the displacement trend:

| Model stage | Slope (mm/added segment) | 95% CI |
|---|---:|---:|
| Original physical, endpoint root | 6.666 | [5.358, 7.955] |
| Chassis-centered, endpoint root | 5.803 | [4.460, 7.128] |

Mass distribution contributes to the dynamics, but chassis mass asymmetry is
neither the unique nor sufficient cause.

### 3.7 Exact mechanical reflection confirmed robot-fixed polarity

The complete model was reflected through the longitudinal origin: positions,
orientations, joint axes, inertia products, and STL triangles were reflected.
Each source trajectory acquired equal-magnitude opposite-sign displacement,
with cancellation residuals on the order of \(10^{-12}\) m or smaller.

This is the strongest evidence that the direction is selected by distributed
fore-aft polar mechanics rather than a world-axis preference. However, the
early reflected models still used a one-ended dynamics tree; therefore this
test established robot-fixed polarity but did not yet quantify tree bias.

### 3.8 The first symmetric primitive null exposed a second problem

A physical primitive null was constructed from centered symmetric shapes with
zero longitudinal COM offset. It preserved measured component masses,
actuator names/order, gains, damping, armature, friction, and control limits.
Its displacement trend was smaller but still positive:

\[
2.263\ [1.187,3.371]\ \mathrm{mm/added\ segment}.
\]

This falsified the hypothesis that geometric and inertial symmetry alone must
produce zero mean. The remaining privileged structure was the one-ended
floating root and its contact-constraint enumeration.

### 3.9 Head/tail re-rooting identified the numerical sign selector

For the symmetric primitive model, head-root and tail-root constructions had
the same physical geometry but produced opposite deterministic displacement:

\[
D_H=+14.2808\ \mathrm{mm},
\qquad
D_T=-14.2045\ \mathrm{mm}.
\]

This directly identified the serial-tree/rooted contact representation as a
nonphysical sign selector. The effect is especially visible when many nearly
coplanar feet compete in an overconstrained frictional contact solve: tiny
height and load differences choose different active support sets and are then
rectified by friction.

### 3.10 The center-rooted symmetric dynamic null passed the null test

The null was rebuilt with a centered freejoint, paired mirror branches,
center-out alternating explicit contact pairs, symmetric initialization, and a
tight Newton/elliptic contact solve. Across 1,280 ordinary IID trials:

- every per-\(N\) 95% confidence interval contained zero;
- the maximum absolute per-\(N\) sample mean was 5.38 mm;
- the grand mean was \(1.54\,[-0.65,3.80]\) mm;
- the fitted slope was
  \(0.044\,[-1.148,1.232]\) mm per added segment.

This is the appropriate single-model empirical null. Individual trials still
move tens of millimeters in either direction, but the distribution is centered
around zero and has no resolved growth of signed mean with \(N\).

For distinction, the earlier source-plus-exact-reflection pooled null has
exactly zero mean by construction. The center-rooted dynamic null does not use
pair cancellation: its near-zero mean is an empirical result from ordinary
single-model simulations.

## 4. Final correction applied to the real STL robot

The null-model corrections were transferred to the real robot without imposing
fore-aft geometric symmetry.

### 4.1 Preserved physical content

The generated models preserve:

- every source STL mesh;
- measured body masses, inertias, and inertial-frame positions;
- joint names, limits, damping, armature, and friction loss;
- actuator names, ordering, gains, force limits, and control limits;
- the \(15^\circ\) body-yaw half-amplitude and the IID leg-pitch process.

When an edge is reversed in the dynamics tree, its world-frame joint anchor is
retained and the local axis is negated so that the original positive controller
coordinate remains physically equivalent.

### 4.2 Three matched representations

For each \(N=4,\ldots,10\), three versions were generated:

- \(H\): head-rooted reference;
- \(C\): center-rooted production model;
- \(T\): tail-rooted reference.

All use the same solver, explicit contact pairs, and symmetric initialization.
The head/center/tail trio shares the same IID seed and control trajectory.

The root-debiased estimator and root-bias amplitude are

\[
D_{\mathrm{debias}}=\frac{D_H+D_T}{2},
\qquad
B_{\mathrm{root}}=\frac{D_H-D_T}{2},
\]

and the center-model representation residual is

\[
E_C=D_C-D_{\mathrm{debias}}.
\]

### 4.3 Structural equivalence

All 21 generated models passed mass, inertia, body/geometry/joint/actuator
count, naming, and actuator-order checks. Across random joint configurations,
the maximum body-position mismatch was \(8.54\times10^{-12}\) m and the
maximum rotation mismatch was \(6.32\times10^{-8}\) rad. All smoke tests
passed with zero MuJoCo warnings.

### 4.4 Matched IID result

There were 32 independent trials for each root and each \(N\), totaling 672
matched simulations. Every trial passed.

| \(N\) | Head mean (mm) | Center mean (mm) | Tail mean (mm) | Debiased mean (mm) | Root bias (mm) | Center residual (mm) |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 4 | 48.15 | 34.69 | 19.94 | 34.05 | 14.10 | 0.64 |
| 5 | 36.88 | 22.83 | 7.31 | 22.09 | 14.79 | 0.74 |
| 6 | 53.45 | 37.72 | 19.27 | 36.36 | 17.09 | 1.36 |
| 7 | 50.68 | 31.13 | 10.57 | 30.63 | 20.05 | 0.50 |
| 8 | 58.82 | 37.54 | 15.02 | 36.92 | 21.90 | 0.62 |
| 9 | 53.60 | 29.15 | 4.30 | 28.95 | 24.65 | 0.20 |
| 10 | 63.11 | 36.20 | 8.76 | 35.94 | 27.17 | 0.27 |

The center model agrees with the endpoint average to within 0.20--1.36 mm in
the IID ensemble means. Relative to the 14.10--27.17 mm root-bias amplitude,
this removes 92.0--99.2% of the first-order representation bias.

The original length-dependent trend is no longer resolved:

| Quantity | Slope (mm/added segment) | 95% bootstrap CI | Interpretation |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Physical head root, corrected solver/contact representation | 2.990 | [0.309, 5.661] | Positive root-dependent trend remains |

## 5. Direct comparison with the dynamic null

The comparison figure displays all three rooted realizations explicitly.
The head-rooted curve lies above the center-rooted result, the tail-rooted
curve lies below it, and their endpoint average is the debiased estimate.
This makes the root-dependent displacement component visible rather than
hiding it inside the debiased estimator.

| \(N\) | Dynamic-null mean (mm) | Real center-root mean (mm) | Real-minus-null (mm) | Difference 95% CI (mm) |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 4 | 0.36 | 34.69 | 34.33 | [16.10, 52.93] |
| 5 | 4.92 | 22.83 | 17.91 | [4.08, 31.84] |
| 6 | -2.94 | 37.72 | 40.66 | [25.25, 56.69] |
| 7 | 5.38 | 31.13 | 25.75 | [12.47, 39.21] |
| 8 | 0.61 | 37.54 | 36.93 | [24.29, 48.08] |
| 9 | -1.29 | 29.15 | 30.44 | [16.05, 44.60] |
| 10 | 3.73 | 36.20 | 32.48 | [18.97, 46.49] |

The difference confidence interval is positive at every tested \(N\). Thus
the retained real-robot mean is not explained by the stochastic IID process
alone, nor by the first-order root bias removed by center-rooting.

The comparison also clarifies what “zero displacement” means for the null:

- an individual null trial can move substantially in either direction;
- its *ensemble mean* is statistically compatible with zero;
- the real model distribution has similar stochastic width but is shifted by
  approximately 31 mm on average.

## 6. Root-cause classification

### Confirmed causes

**C1. One-ended dynamics-tree/contact-order bias.** Head/tail re-rooting
changes the signed result while preserving physical geometry. Its amplitude
increases strongly with \(N\). Center-rooting removes 92--99% of this
first-order component.

**C2. Distributed fore-aft physical-model polarity under friction.** Exact
mechanical reflection reverses the displacement to machine precision, while
world rotation does not reverse body-frame forward motion. Tangential friction
is necessary. The retained STL and finite-chain boundary assemblies therefore
provide a real model-level direction selector.

**C3. Intermittent contact rectification.** Microscopic clearance and load
differences switch the active contact set. Extension and contraction then
occur under non-equivalent support and slip states. The IID up/down process
gates this mechanism but is not a signed forward/backward input.

### Contributors, but not sufficient explanations

- distributed mass first moment and terminal missing-link mass;
- shoulder and yaw-link placement asymmetry;
- joint compliance, armature, and contact regularization;
- finite-chain head/tail boundary conditions;
- initial global body-wave phase, which changes magnitude but does not select
  the sign in the tested endpoint-rooted null.

### Rejected as primary causes

- world-axis or checkerboard preference;
- static numerical drift or inherited initialization momentum;
- nested random streams across \(N\);
- non-foot chassis/shoulder ground contact;
- foot-patch shape or foot-tip fore-aft offset alone;
- chassis mass asymmetry alone;
- rolling/torsional or passive joint friction alone;
- IID sampling itself as a source of a compulsory positive mean.

## 7. What the remaining real-robot displacement may represent

The remaining positive center/debiased mean should be treated as a
**calibrated physical-model effect with a bounded numerical uncertainty**, not
as either proven hardware locomotion or residual IID bias.

The hypotheses are ranked below.

### H1 - Distributed morphology/contact rectification (strong support)

The real model has several irreducible fore-aft differences: `Chasis1.STL`
differs from repeated chassis geometry; the terminal segment lacks an outgoing
link pair; shoulders and yaw links contribute strongly to the longitudinal
mass first moment; and the two boundaries are mechanically different. Exact
reflection reverses the sign, and tangential-friction removal suppresses the
motion. This is currently the leading explanation.

### H2 - Residual nonlinear solver/tree sensitivity (quantified confound)

The center-root IID mean differs from the head/tail average by only
0.20--1.36 mm, but a deterministic all-down phase condition produced a maximum
7.31 mm residual. A finite serial-coordinate and finite ordered constraint
representation cannot be claimed to be mathematically root-invariant during
non-smooth multi-contact. This residual is smaller than the 22--37 mm physical
mean, but it must remain in the uncertainty budget.

### H3 - Finite-chain and odd/even phase structure (moderate support)

After root debiasing, the mean alternates rather than increases monotonically:
even \(N\) values tend to be near 36--37 mm and odd values near 22--31 mm.
With spatial phase increment \(-\pi\), adding one segment changes the terminal
phase class and boundary loading. This may create a genuine odd/even effect,
but 32 trials per \(N\) are insufficient to resolve it cleanly.

### H4 - Actuator/compliance interaction with asymmetric morphology (open)

Armature, damping, tracking error, and compliant contact are preserved because
they belong to the measured robot model. Prior ablations show that changing
compliance affects magnitude. Their interaction with asymmetric boundary
geometry has not yet been separated class by class.

### H5 - Sim-to-real mismatch (open)

Even a physically interpretable simulation effect is not yet a hardware
validation. Floor friction, joint zero offsets, backlash, cable routing,
individual motor gains, and actual foot compliance may reinforce, weaken, or
reverse the simulated residual.

## 8. Important cross-stage caveat

The deterministic all-down displacement changed substantially when the contact
representation and solver were rewritten between the earlier causal audit and
the final center-root audit, including a sign change in the reported world
axis. This is evidence that the absolute deterministic baseline is sensitive
to contact representation. It does not invalidate the final IID root
comparison because the head/center/tail models in Step 26 are matched within
one solver/contact formulation. It does mean that absolute deterministic
values from different steps must not be treated as directly interchangeable
physical measurements.

For this reason, the strongest conclusions in this report are based on
symmetry pairs and matched within-stage contrasts, not on raw differences
between historical steps.

## 9. Recommended next experiments

1. **Reflect the optimized center-rooted real model.** Run exact mechanical
   source/reflection pairs under the final Step-26 contact representation and
   matched IID seeds. If morphology is the remaining sign source,
   \(D_{\mathrm{real}}+D_{\mathrm{mirror}}\) should be near zero while each
   model retains a nonzero mean of opposite sign.
2. **Component-class reflection/ablation.** In the center-rooted model,
   independently symmetrize contact geometry and inertial placement for
   chassis, shoulders, yaw links, pitch links, and terminal assemblies. This
   will localize the 31 mm real-minus-null shift.
3. **Solver/contact-order envelope.** Repeat a smaller matched ensemble over
   timestep, solver tolerance, contact softness, and several balanced contact
   permutations. Report the resulting range as numerical uncertainty.
4. **Increase physical-model trials.** Raise the center/head/tail ensemble
   from 32 to at least 128 seeds per \(N\) before claiming an odd/even effect.
5. **Hardware calibration.** Compare the same command histories on flat ground,
   rotate the physical robot/environment orientation, and measure COM motion,
   individual foot contacts, motor tracking, and floor friction.

## 10. Production protocol for subsequent noise/redundancy studies

1. Use the center-rooted physical model as the primary simulation.
2. Keep strict-independent random streams across \(N\).
3. Preserve matched head/center/tail seeds for a smaller audit subset.
4. Report
   \(D_{\mathrm{debias}}=(D_H+D_T)/2\),
   \(B_{\mathrm{root}}=(D_H-D_T)/2\), and \(E_C\).
5. Evaluate locomotion reliability relative to the corresponding no-noise
   physical baseline; do not interpret the sign of raw displacement as an IID
   randomness effect.
6. Treat a root-bias or center-residual change as a model-validation warning,
   not as a redundancy result.

## Final scientific statement

The corrected model does **not** support the claim that increasing segment
number intrinsically creates an increasing positive mean displacement under
IID random contact. That trend disappears after the dynamics tree is centered
or endpoint-debiased. The symmetric dynamic null fluctuates around zero. The
real asymmetric robot remains shifted away from that null, indicating a
separate morphology/contact rectification effect whose magnitude is
approximately 31 mm under the present protocol and whose numerical
root/solver uncertainty is now explicitly measured rather than hidden.
